Who is this for?

First: what aphasia is.

After a stroke, some people experience aphasia: difficulty using or understanding language. Someone may be able to express a few keywords but struggle to form a sentence.

Aphasia is a language disorder. It is more specific than a general “speech impediment.” Abilities vary widely from person to person.

Our intended first users

Adults with nonfluent aphasia

People who can contribute a few words—spoken or typed—and review a suggested message, independently or with support.

We are exploring support for that workflow. We are not claiming Kendrick fits every person with aphasia, stuttering, or pronunciation difficulty.

Plain-language framing guided by NIH/NIDCD descriptions of aphasia. Suitability depends on individual language, reading, vision, and motor abilities.

Say the NIH line slowly. Emphasize review/verify. Do not imitate aphasic speech.

06 / 11

The person stays in control

Kendrick may suggest.
It does not decide.

Edit the draft

Every suggested sentence stays fully editable before anything is spoken or shared.

Reject and restart

The person can discard a draft, return to their words, or start over.

Keep original words

Source fragments remain visible beside the suggestion when they differ.

Clarify when unsure

Ambiguous inputs can trigger one question instead of a guessed sentence. This detector is imperfect—some ambiguous cases still slip through.

No automatic speaking or sending. Clarification is a safety behavior in progress, not a solved clinical guarantee.

Be honest about clarification misses. Control is the product claim, not perfect AI.

Where Kendrick fits

Related tools. Different job.

Wispr Flow

Spoken input → polished writing across apps.

Strong for dictation and drafting text. Overlap exists when speech becomes writing; the primary job is writing productivity.

AAC apps

Build messages with symbols, grids, prediction, or AI assists.

Established communication systems (e.g. Spoken and many others) already help people compose and speak messages. Kendrick does not replace them.

Kendrick

A few words → reviewed everyday message → optional speak.

A focused prototype for keyword-to-message review in conversation. Worth testing with this audience—not a claim that nobody else helps people communicate.

Based on Wispr Flow and public AAC product positioning. No “first,” superiority, or complete-sentence prerequisite claims.

If asked about Wispr: overlap is real; our pitch is reviewed spoken output for aphasia-focused keyword starts.

What we test next

Does it preserve meaning?
Does it reduce effort?

Questions to answer

When someone supplies keywords, does the draft match their intended meaning? Do they need fewer struggles to participate in an everyday request? Do they feel in control of what is spoken?

How we answer them

Co-design and evaluate with people with aphasia and speech-language pathologists. Measure edits, rejected drafts, clarification usefulness, and perceived control—before any accuracy or outcome claims.

No invented users, partners, hospitals, or success rates.

End this slide by inviting collaboration, not by fabricating traction.
